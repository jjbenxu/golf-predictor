How this works, and how far to trust it

  • Score. Course difficulty, plus the effect of wind, rain and temperature, plus how many strokes better or worse than an average tour entrant the player has been lately (recent rounds count more).
  • Odds. The tournament is played 10,000 times on your device against the players who entered the most recent edition at that course. Every player gets realistic round-to-round luck.
  • Winning the round means posting the lowest score of the day, including ties.
  • Wind direction is optional. Each course has its own pattern, learned from past rounds. At most courses it changes little; at a few exposed ones it is worth up to half a stroke in a strong wind. Leave it unspecified to assume the usual wind for that course.
  • Weather moves the score more than the odds. Everyone plays in the same conditions, and the data showed no reliable sign that particular players gain or lose in wind. Wind does add randomness, which slightly trims favourites' chances.
  • Golf is mostly luck over one round. Skill explains only a small share of a single round, so even the best player in the world rarely tops 20% to win. Tested on the 2026 season, players the model gave a 60 to 80% chance of making the cut made it about that often.
  • The model does not know about injuries, course history or current form beyond results. For fun only, not betting advice.
